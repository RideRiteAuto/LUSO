# Navora gathering-resource art direction

## Core identity

Begin with recognizable reality. Pine must read as a real pine, copper as copper-bearing
weathered rock, and a berry bush as a plausible living plant. Navora's visual signature is
grounded maritime fantasy: natural forms, restrained stylization, rich but plausible color,
weathering, useful silhouettes, and no blocky voxel language.

## Shape economy

- Spend polygons on the outer silhouette, major branch/rock planes, trunk flare, exposed roots,
  mineral protrusions, and interactable features.
- Move bark fissures, leaf veins, small fractures, needles, and pitting into albedo/normal/ORM.
- Preserve slight asymmetry, gravity, exposure, competition, breakage, and age.
- Avoid procedural sameness: three family variants need distinct primary structure.

## Gameplay readability

- Validate a flat-color silhouette at the normal approach distance.
- Use material color as a secondary signal: copper-orange with limited green oxidation; cool pale
  tin seams; red berry clusters; pale birch bark; deep evergreen pine mass.
- Avoid neon outlines, fantasy glow, uniform ore polka dots, and metallic host rock.
- Make young, standard, and mature variants related but instantly non-identical.

## Initial family anchors

- **Pine:** Scots-pine-adjacent reddish plated bark, irregular tiered crown, visible branch rhythm,
  deep-green needle clusters, 12–24 m review range across variants.
- **Copper:** warm gray-brown host rock, copper-orange seams/plates, sparse green oxidation, rounded
  weathered fracture; low mound and upright cluster variants.
- **Tin:** cooler charcoal/slate host rock, pale silver-gray quartz/cassiterite-like seams, sharper
  broken planes than copper, no copper oxidation colors.
- **Redberry (provisional):** multi-stem woody shrub, irregular serrated leaf masses, clustered red
  fruit retained into LOD1/LOD2, 0.8–1.7 m review range.
